Research and three directions for the new site

I studied 20 live sites (Awwwards Architecture winners plus leading practices and a stone brand), screened the full React Bits catalogue (about 200 components) and measured every candidate's real size, read the company PDF, and audited the photo archive. Everything below is measured or fingerprinted from the live sites, not recalled.

What I need from you

  1. Pick a direction (A, B or C below, or a named mix). My recommendation is at the end of the directions.
  2. Confirm the project count. The company PDF says “over 51 projects”; your brief says about 21 recorded. The site will only claim what the project list supports.
  3. Confirm three facts from the PDF: “more than 40 years”; the second phone number (PDF: 92278 66634/35, brief: 66635 only); the grandfather’s name as printed, “Parshottamdas Keshavlalbha[i] Sompura”.

Ruled out, and why

Tech or game feel: Hyperspeed, Laser Flow, Electric Border, Glitch Text, Decrypted Text, Faulty Terminal, Letter Glitch, Pixel Blast, Pixel Trail, CRT Warp, Scanner, Radar, Evil Eye, Ballpit, Lanyard, Splash Cursor, Blob Cursor, Target Cursor, all cursor trails.

Neon, chrome or synthetic colour that won’t recolour to stone: Iridescence, Liquid Chrome, Molten Metal, Metallic Paint, Prismatic Burst, Plasma, Aurora, Grainient (purple-first; Noise does the grain job at 0.5 kB).

Cartoon bounce: Bounce Cards, Gooey Nav, Elastic Slider, Falling Text, Sticker Peel, Curved Loop (reads as a startup marquee), all of the “Micro” set.

Too heavy for their job: Silk (pulls in React Three Fiber, 246 kB total), Model Viewer (309 kB; we’ll write our own three.js scene), Dome Gallery and Circular Gallery (spectacle over content).

Doesn’t render reliably: Side Rays, Dark Veil and Micro Slats showed nothing in the demo pane; Light Rays did the same because it waits for visibility, which I confirmed in its source.

Variant: TypeScript + Tailwind for every component, to match the recommended stack. Each is recoloured to the palette through props or CSS variables; none keep their default purples.

Three directions

Direction A

The Mountain Opens

Cinematic and dark-first. The site opens on a night stage where an artificial mountain sits under dusk light; scrolling splits the rock and walks you inside to a white-stone shrine lit in saffron. The firm’s unique work is the first thing anyone sees.

Structure

Mountain hero (3D, pinned) → “Where Devotion Meets Stone” → three doorways: Fero · Stone · CNC (the Makhno idea) → project index → Palitana workshop and contact.

Drawn from

Igloo Inc. (structure inside a mountain), Getty/Gehry (chaptered 3D story), Sobha Privy (sculpture on a dark stage), Makhno (doorways as navigation).

3D and motion

Mountain reveal as the centrepiece; Light Rays in saffron inside the shrine; Topography behind Fero Works; Split Text headings; Noise over dark sections.

Type

Libre Caslon Display for headlines, Hanken Grotesk for text, IBM Plex Mono for captions (stone, place, dimensions).

Palette (dark-first, measured)

night #0E0E1F · base navy #262654 · panels sand text 16.65:1 sky text 12.66:1 saffron text 6.53:1 slate lines 5.35:1

Weight: first view is HTML + a poster image (about 120 KB AVIF) + two font files; the 3D chunk (187 kB JS + ≤ 400 KB assets) loads after. Risk: the heaviest of the three, and the one most likely to struggle on a mid-range phone, so the poster has to be excellent on its own.

Direction B

Carved in Sandstone

Light, material and craft-led. Pale sandstone ground and navy ink, with type and relief that look cut rather than printed. The 3D is a real P.K. Sompura panel in raking light; a CNC toolpath carves it live. The mountain arrives as the big chapter halfway down.

Structure

Carved-panel hero (3D relief, light follows scroll) → Stone Works → CNC Works (toolpath carving) → Fero Works (Gulbarga photo, then the build sequence in Scroll Stack) → project index → contact.

Drawn from

MERSI (letters cut into the page), Salvatori (stone under raking light), Marvell Tile & Stone (trade firm as design practice), Lyon Béton (material restraint, triangle crop).

3D and motion

Relief and toolpath (one mesh, one texture); Masked Heading with sandstone texture in the letters; Line Waves behind CNC; Scroll Reveal on the family paragraph; Scroll Stack for the mountain sequence; Count Up; Gradual Blur.

Type

Libre Caslon Display, cut large for headings; Hanken Grotesk text; IBM Plex Mono captions.

Palette (light-first, measured)

sand #F4EFE6 · base stone #E7DFD2 · panels navy text 12.33:1 deep slate text 5.65:1 saffron text #985A1F 4.80:1 logo saffron · ornament only

Weight: the lightest 3D (about 150 kB JS + about 150 KB texture); the mountain chapter can use photographs, or add the mountain scene later. Risk: the mountain isn’t the opening shot, so the differentiator depends on the chapter being strong.

Direction C

The Temple Register

Editorial and archive-led. The work is presented as a register of temples: every project listed with place, deity or community, and what the firm did (design, carving, CNC, construction, mountain), whether or not a photo exists. 3D is limited to one mountain chapter.

Structure

One large sentence over the Gulbarga photograph (the Snøhetta pacing) → the register as coloured file tabs (Mosby’s idea) with filters by place and scope → mountain chapter (3D) → the workshop in Palitana → contact.

Drawn from

Mosby’s Files (tab index), Herzog & de Meuron (searchable archive), Snøhetta (one sentence, 0.26 MB JS), Studio Mumbai (place as identity).

3D and motion

Mountain reveal in one chapter; Flowing Menu for the register rows; Staggered Menu on phones; Split Text; Count Up. Least motion of the three.

Type

Libre Caslon Display; Hanken Grotesk; IBM Plex Mono doing more work here (register columns).

Palette (tabs in the four logo colours, measured)

navy tab, sand text 12.33:1 sky tab, navy text 9.38:1 saffron tab, navy text 4.84:1 sand tab, navy text 12.33:1 slate · rules only (3.96:1 on navy)

Weight: lightest overall; the register is plain HTML. Risk: the most conventional of the three; the 3D requirement is met by one chapter only.

Recommendation

Direction B, with A’s mountain as its one big 3D chapter, and C’s register for the projects.

B gives the site a material identity no template has (their own carving, lit and cut live) at the lowest weight, which matters on a mid-range phone over 4G. Putting A’s mountain reveal in the Fero Works chapter gives the differentiator a real 3D moment without making the whole first impression depend on WebGL. C’s register solves the projects that have no photographs. If you want the mountain to be the first thing people see, choose A instead; everything else carries over.

Proposed stack and hosting

Build

  • Astro with React islands: pages ship as HTML with zero JS by default, and each React Bits component or 3D scene hydrates only when visible. React (67 kB) loads only with the first island.
  • TypeScript + Tailwind (and the matching React Bits variant).
  • three.js (vanilla), GSAP + ScrollTrigger + SplitText, Lenis: the combination the winners use.
  • Projects as a typed content collection in the repo (21 or 51 entries): no database needed to show them.

Hosting

  • Vercel: keep for the static site. Its Root Directory still points at frontend/ and must be changed before the first build.
  • Render: likely drop. A static site has no server to run.
  • Supabase: only if you want enquiries stored. A contact form can go through a Vercel function to email instead. We decide at the deployment step, where I’ll list exactly what gets dropped before anything is wiped.
  • Measuring: I can’t hold a phone. I’ll measure load and frame timing with WebPageTest on a mid-range Android profile over 4G, plus Chrome remote debugging on an Android phone of yours over USB, and report the numbers.

If a direction includes a map of projects, its India outline will come from a Survey of India–compliant source that includes all of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh to about 37.1°N. I’ll check the northern extent of the file before using it.
